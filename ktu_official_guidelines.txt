1. Introduction to the Activity Points System (2024 Scheme)
Higher education is undergoing a profound transformation as
industries, communities, and global systems increasingly expect graduates to demonstrate
competencies that extend far beyond academic excellence or technical mastery. Today’s
professionals must be effective communicators, collaborative team members, creative problem-
solvers, socially responsible citizens, and adaptable innovators capable of responding to rapidly
evolving technological and societal challenges. Alongside strong domain knowledge, skills such as

leadership, empathy, entrepreneurial thinking, ethical responsibility, and community engagement
have become essential attributes for success.
Recognising these evolving expectations, APJ Abdul Kalam Technological University (APJAKTU)
has implemented a comprehensive Activity Points System under the 2024 Curriculum Scheme for
B. Tech, B. Des, BBA, and BCA programmes. This framework provides structured opportunities
for students to build personal, professional, and societal competencies that complement their
academic learning. Through participation in a wide range of co-curricular and extra-curricular
activities including sports, arts, NSS/NCC, community service, cultural programmes, technical
competitions, research, innovation, entrepreneurship, industry interaction, professional body
activities, and approved skilling courses students gain experiences that enrich and enhance their
holistic development.
To promote balanced and meaningful engagement, every student must earn a minimum of 120
Activity Points during the programme. These points are distributed across three Activity Groups,
and students must earn at least 40 points from each Group to fulfil the requirement of 3 Activity
Credits mandated by the curriculum.
Additional point requirements apply to specific categories of students:
1. B. Tech Lateral Entry Students:
Must earn a minimum of 90 Activity Points, including at least 30 points from each Group.
2. PwD (Persons with Disabilities) Students:
Must earn a minimum of 60 Activity Points, including at least 20 points from each Group.
The Activity Points framework offers students the flexibility to choose activities based on their
interests and strengths and to complete them across semesters at their own pace. By the final year,
students are expected to have developed a balanced portfolio of technical expertise, leadership,
teamwork experience, creativity, innovation capability, and social responsibility qualities essential
for contributing to national development and global progress.
Institutions play an important role in ensuring smooth implementation. Colleges must verify all
supporting documents, consolidate Activity Points every semester, and upload the summary to the
KTU portal within the prescribed period. All documentation must be authenticated by the Senior
Faculty Advisor (SFA) and retained for verification by the University’s Academic Auditors.
A detailed list of Activity Groups, Activity Heads, and Sub-Activity Heads is provided in
Table- 2, and students are encouraged to plan their participation throughout their academic journey.

2. General Guidelines and Rules for Activity Points
i. Participation points and winning points cannot be combined for the same event.
If a student wins a position, only the points for winning will be awarded.
The highest level of achievement attained will be considered.
Example: If a student wins at the College Level and later at the State Level, only the State
Level points will be counted.
ii. Each activity has a maximum point limit, as specified in the Activity Points Table.
Students cannot claim points beyond the maximum limit prescribed for any segment.
iii. The Activity Points Table lists all approved activity categories under each Group and specifies
the minimum and maximum points that may be earned.
Points will be awarded based on:
a. Merit of participation
b. Level of achievement (College / Zonal / State / National / International)
c. Compliance with certification and verification requirements
iv. A total of 3 Activity Credits is mandatory for the award of the Degree.
Activity Points are compulsory, and 3 credits are awarded upon earning the minimum required
points.
Activity Points and Activity Credits are not considered for SGPA or CGPA calculation.
v. Earning the required Activity Points is compulsory for the award of the degree.
Completion of academic coursework alone is not sufficient. The degree will not be awarded
unless the minimum Activity Points are achieved.
The final semester results may be withheld until the student completes the required Activity
Points.
vi. Only activities completed during the period of study of the programme are eligible for
Activity Points. Certificates or achievements obtained before joining the programme will not
be considered.
vii. Submission of Fake or Fraudulent Documents
If, at any stage, the University identifies that a student has submitted fake, falsified, or
fraudulent documents for the purpose of claiming Activity Points, the Activity Points awarded
to the student shall be cancelled, and strict disciplinary action will be initiated against the
student with the approval of the appropriate statutory bodies of the University.

3. Conclusion
The Activity Points System is an integral component of the 2024 curriculum, designed to complement
academic learning by fostering well-rounded student development. By engaging in the approved
activity categories, students cultivate essential skills and attributes that enhance their personal growth,
professional readiness, and social responsibility.
This framework reflects the University’s commitment to nurturing graduates who are capable,
confident, ethical, and prepared to meet the expectations of industry and society. The system also
empowers students to explore diverse interests, develop new capabilities, and build meaningful
experiences throughout their academic journey.
The effective implementation of the Activity Points System requires close cooperation among
students, faculty, and institutions. Institutions play a critical role in guiding, verifying, and supporting
students throughout this process, with clear documentation and verification mechanisms ensuring
transparency, fairness, and academic integrity across all activity groups.
Any revisions or enhancements to this Activity Points framework will be introduced in subsequent
academic years with the approval of the Academic Council of the University.
